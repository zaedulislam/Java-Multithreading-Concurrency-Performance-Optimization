# Race Condition

![](https://github.com/zaedulislam/Java-Multithreading-Concurrency-Performance-Optimization/blob/main/images/image_20.1.png)


We already mentioned one race condition where two threads were accessing the shared variable. In the e-commerce inventory counter example, two threads were executing non-atomic operations on a shared resource called `items`, and there was a race between the last operations of the incrementing thread and the decrementing thread.

We saw that if the decrementing thread is scheduled before the incrementing thread, the result is one.

![](https://github.com/zaedulislam/Java-Multithreading-Concurrency-Performance-Optimization/blob/main/images/image_20.2.png)


We saw that if the Decrementing thread is scheduled before the incrementing thread, the result is one. And if the decrementing thread is scheduled after the incrementing thread for the last operation, then the result is negative one.

![](https://github.com/zaedulislam/Java-Multithreading-Concurrency-Performance-Optimization/blob/main/images/image_20.3.png)

## Race Condition - Solution



![](https://github.com/zaedulislam/Java-Multithreading-Concurrency-Performance-Optimization/blob/main/images/image_20.4.png)

In the case of the ecommerce example, the critical sections were the items and the items minus minus, and we solve the race condition by declaring the increment and decrement methods synchronized.

![](https://github.com/zaedulislam/Java-Multithreading-Concurrency-Performance-Optimization/blob/main/images/image_20.5.png)
